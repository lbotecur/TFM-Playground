# Toward a second model: what limits the first one, and how we test it

Written on 2026-10-08, before any of the runs below. The paper models (`graph_scm_base_w5000/epoch_100`,
`graph_scm_base_w5000_omics/epoch_30`, `graph_scm_base/epoch_100`) stay as they are; this is the research for
a next version.

## What we know

- **Interactions.** On synthetic tasks with 300 training rows and no noise, w5000 reaches AUROC 0.94 on the
  XOR of 2 features and 0.59 on the XOR of 3 (TabPFN-3.5 0.997, TabPFN-Wide 0.981, XGBoost 0.982, TabICL v2
  with one estimator 0.990). It is our weakness on madelon (0.67 vs Wide 0.94), and plausibly on PCMAC and
  RELATHE, the HDLSS datasets with the most rows.
- **They are learned, late.** Along the base run, XOR-3 stays at chance up to epoch 75 and then rises
  (0.47, 0.42, 0.51, 0.59 at epochs 25, 50, 75, 100; 0.63 and 0.64 at 125 and 145 of the batch-32
  continuation). XOR-2 rises earlier (0.65 at epoch 25, 0.98 at 100). The linear task is learned by epoch 25.
- **Widening erodes them.** w5000 epoch 50 vs epoch 100: XOR-2 0.98 -> 0.94, XOR-3 0.64 -> 0.59.
- **Training was limited by the prior's generation, not the GPU.** 1.36 s of each 1.8 s step was the CPU
  generating the 8 tables on one thread (`n_jobs=1`). That is also why batch 32 took 4x longer per epoch.
- **Our prior is TabICLv2's**, whose defaults reproduce the prior TabICLv2 was trained with, including tables
  of up to 1024 rows. We cut it to 40-300 rows, log-uniform: a median of ~110 rows, and with the 0.3-0.9 train
  fraction a typical context of ~65 rows.
- **The comparison of base epoch 145 vs 100 on MLOmics** (7 of 10 lost, -0.0025 AUROC) is not evidence that
  more training hurts: a base model (at most 100 features in training) was judged on tables of thousands of
  features, already saturated, and the difference is within noise. Bases are judged with the fixed
  validation below.

## Hypotheses

- **H1, context size (main).** A PFN learns the posterior predictive for the contexts it is trained on. With
  ~65 context rows a 3-way interaction is almost unidentifiable, so ignoring it is close to optimal, and that
  is what the model learned. Larger training tables (up to 1024 rows) should teach it to use interactions
  when the data supports them. Consistent with: the late and slow rise of XOR-3, the erosion by widening
  (thousands of columns, few rows), TabPFN-Wide solving XOR-3 although trained on 40-300 rows (it inherits
  TabPFN v2, trained on larger tables), and our losses concentrating on the HDLSS datasets with most rows.
- **H2, batch diversity.** With `batch_size_per_gp` = batch size, the 8 tables of a step share one sample of
  the prior's hyperparameters: one kind of problem per step, noisy and correlated gradients. TabICL uses 4 per
  group in much larger batches.
- **H3, mechanisms.** In a random graph_scm, pure interactions (XOR-like, no marginal effect) are rare.
  Sampling tree, product and quadratic functions more often should make them more frequent.
- **H4, volume.** ~0.8M tables (base) is far below TabICL and TabPFN. Generation in parallel gives ~4x more
  tables per GPU-hour at no other change.

## Instruments (this PR)

- `--workers N` in `pretrain.py`: tables generated by N processes; `steps.csv` per run records the tables
  trained on and the seconds spent waiting for the prior (`experiments/prior_throughput.py` measures the
  generation rate per number of workers).
- `--batch-size-per-gp`, `--graph-fct-types`, `--prior-type a+b` with `--prior-weights`, `--no-log-seq-len`.
- `experiments/validate_run.py` (`tfmplayground/validation.py`): fixed validation of every snapshot,
  resumable and able to follow runs while they train:
  - Loss on 200 held-out tables per set: graph_scm with 100, 300 and 1000 rows (context 70%), graph_scm with
    300 rows widened by 2000 features, and tree_scm with 300 rows. Same files for every run.
  - Probes, 5 seeds, 500 test rows: XOR-2 and XOR-3 with 100, 300 and 1000 training rows; XOR-3 with 1000 rows
    and 20 noise features; linear with 300 rows and 480 noise features; madelon's generator with 1000 rows,
    without and with 480 noise features.

## Rules

1. Choices are made on the fixed validation only. The paper benchmarks (MLOmics, HDLSS, Shamir, TabArena) are
   run once, on the final model of this research, and reported with the first model.
2. Runs are compared at an equal number of tables trained on (from `steps.csv`), not epochs.
3. Every run keeps its launch command and commit (`args_<time>.json`).

## Plan

**Step 1, cheap, no training.** The current checkpoints (base epochs 25-145, w5000 epochs 50 and 100) and
TabPFN-3.5, TabPFN-Wide, XGBoost and random forest through the probes, which now include 1000 training rows.
If TabPFN goes to ~1.0 on XOR-3 with 1000 rows and ours stays near 0.6, our model does not use the extra
evidence, as H1 predicts.

**Step 2, screening from scratch.** graph_scm, 2-100 features, batch 8 x 1, lr 1e-4, 1.2M tables each (150
epochs of 1000 steps), one GPU per arm, the workers each needs:

| Arm | Rows | Change | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 40-300 | none (the base recipe) | control |
| B | 40-1024 | | H1 |
| C | 40-1024 | `--batch-size-per-gp 2` | H2 |
| D | 40-1024 | `--graph-fct-types default,tree,prod,quad` | H3 |
| E | 40-1024 | `--no-log-seq-len` (more large tables) | H1, strength |

Decision, fixed now:
- Primary: mean held-out loss over graph_r100, graph_r300 and graph_r1000 at 1.2M tables.
- An arm improves on another if it is better at each of the last three snapshots (0.96, 1.08 and 1.2M
  tables), not at a single point.
- Secondary, to explain: XOR-3 with 1000 and 300 rows, XOR-2 with 300 rows, linear with 480 noise features.
- An arm that improves the primary but loses more than 0.01 of loss on graph_r100 is not taken as it is: small
  tables are most of our real use.

**Step 3.** The improving changes together, trained long (>= 5M tables), checked against the criteria below.

**Step 4, widening without erosion.** From step 3's base, with the validation following the run from the first
snapshot: the base's metrics (graph_r100-1000, XOR) must not fall while graph_r300_wide2000 improves. Options
to compare if they fall: more batches without widening, a lower lr, and larger tables in the batches without
widening.

**Targets for the final model** (validation only): XOR-3 with 1000 rows >= 0.90, XOR-2 with 300 rows >= 0.98,
linear with 480 noise features >= 0.95, and held-out loss no worse than w5000 epoch 100 on any set.
